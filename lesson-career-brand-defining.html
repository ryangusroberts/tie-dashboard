Part 1: Defining Personal Executive Brand

In this training we're going to define your personal executive brand. This is how people will think about you when you're not in the room. Let's dive in.

Definition: A personal executive brand is a strategic process where individuals, especially those in leadership roles, intentionally create and manage a public image that reflects their unique value, expertise, and professional reputation. It's how we establish a distinctive identity that goes beyond just their job title or company affiliation.

While everyone has a personal brand, not everyone actively manages theirs. You can claim that you don't believe in having a personal brand, and that will become your brand.

The purpose of a strong personal executive brand

  • Clarity & Consistency in your communications about who you are and what you stand for
  • Differentiation, standing out from the crowd by highlighting your uniqueness
  • Strategic Direction, knowing, and letting others know, where you want your career to go
  • Networking & Opportunities, a strong brand can open doors that you never knew existed
  • Trust & Credibility, when you show up the same way every time, people start to believe that's the real you

Elements of a solid personal executive brand

  • Brand Voice, the words and language you use; the way you speak and write
  • Unique Value Proposition, what makes you distinct and different from everyone else
  • Mission & Vision Statements, understanding what your mission in life is, and what vision you have for the world
  • Beliefs, what you believe in; what you stand for
  • Leadership Style, how you lead your teams speaks volumes of your character
  • Authenticity, obviously a hallmark of this program; never trying to be something you're not

Part 3: The 3 Pillars of Your Attractive Character

We tend to know, like, and trust people who seem to share more about themselves than just a one-dimensional perspective. If all I ever talked about was just Leadership, my message might reach lots of people but they might not get a sense of who I am, and that would lead them to wonder about my ulterior motives.

My three pillars

  • Leadership: I talk about leadership more than anything else. It's my Ikigai (life purpose) and the whole reason I do all of this.
  • Family: I love to share the successes and stories of my wife and children, as well as my friends and colleagues. I especially love sharing our global travel and adventures in Colorado.
  • Health: I talk about my fight against Leukemia and my long recovery process, primarily because it changed my entire life and world perspective, but also because it's a way to relate to people who are also facing tough challenges in their lives.

Define your three pillars

Write down three themes that are important to you: one that is business-related (Business), one that is exciting and interesting to you (Passion), and one that is personal (Personal). You can choose any three, but at least one should be somewhat personal or unique to you. For me, my health journey is somewhat intimate, but I do this to be seen as a whole human being, complete with strengths and flaws.

As you create content, try to incorporate these three pillars so that you are talking more about these than other topics. Aim for 60% around your business pillar, and 20% each for your passion and personal pillars.

Part 5: Crafting Your Elevator Pitch

A well-structured elevator pitch is concise, engaging, and clearly communicates who you are, what you do, and the value you offer, all within 30 to 60 seconds. Here is how to craft a compelling elevator pitch about yourself:

  1. Introduction (who you are): Start by briefly introducing yourself or your company. Include your name, job title, or business name and a brief statement of your professional identity.
  2. What you do, or the problem you solve: Clearly explain what you or your business does in simple, non-technical language. Focus on the problem you solve for your target audience and why it matters.
  3. Unique selling proposition (what makes you different): Highlight what sets you apart from others in your field. Identify your unique strengths, approach, or past successes that show your value.
  4. Value proposition (the benefit or outcome): Share the tangible results or benefits your audience can expect from working with you. Focus on measurable outcomes that demonstrate your impact.
Example: "Hi, my name is Jane Doe, and I'm a digital marketing strategist with 10 years of experience helping small businesses grow their online presence. I specialize in creating targeted ad campaigns that drive traffic and increase sales. My work is unique in that it is data-driven, which has helped my clients improve their ROI by an average of 25%." Can your elevator pitch be shorter? Yes. Yes it can.

Key tips: Keep it concise (30-60 seconds). Focus on value, highlight the benefit or impact of what you do, not just the tasks. Adapt to your audience. Practice: rehearse your pitch to ensure it sounds natural and confident.

Storytelling: answering the "what do you do?" question

Here's the easiest "What do you do?" storytelling framework: "You know when ______ [the problem]? That's what I do [your solution], I do B [action], and C happens [desired outcome], plus the cost of inaction."

  • "You know when technology companies are growing and need to hire top engineers quickly? That's what I do. I meet with hiring leaders to forecast project resource needs, then I guide expert recruiters to locate top candidates for them to hire. This keeps their projects on schedule and helps reduce burnout of their current staff."
  • "Highly skilled employees wonder why they are not getting considered for leadership positions. That's what I do. I work with mid-level professionals on mindset shifts, understanding the value of personal branding and learning a higher level of communication that leaders already have. This helps them get unstuck and move up the career ladder."
  • "You know when your dog walks in with mud up to his eyeballs (and a smile on his face) ready to shake wet mud on your clean walls? That's what I do. I provide expert grooming and doggie spa treatments to make your little mud magnet ready for his Hollywood debut."
  • "I help runners increase their performance and duration. I'm a podiatrist."
  • "I help make wedding memories by providing decadent food for up to 500 guests."
  • "You know when you go to the drug store to pick up a prescription? I'm a software developer that focuses on systems that handle those transactions. It also flags any prescriptions that can't be taken with your current prescriptions."
