Decision Velocity

This section delivered by Ryan Roberts

Purpose: Address the psychology of committing to a direction, and remove the "I need more information" excuse.

Why committing feels hard

The biological resistance. Your brain is hardwired to view uncertainty as a physical threat. When you hesitate to commit, that is your amygdala prioritizing survival over the strategic growth your business requires.

The optionality trap. Keeping your options open feels like staying flexible, but in entrepreneurship it is a resource drain. Every unmade decision consumes mental bandwidth that should go to execution.

The cost of stagnation. Staying safe has a price tag. While you wait for the perfect moment, the market moves, competitors gain ground, and your momentum atrophies, which makes the eventual start harder.

Keeping options open feels safer, but it costs momentum.

The 70% rule

The information sweet spot. High-level executives (at companies like Amazon, and in the military) operate on the 70% rule. If you wait for 100% certainty, you are no longer a leader, you are a historian.

Market-derived data. The final 30% of certainty cannot be found in a book or a course. It only exists in the real-world feedback you receive once your offer is live.

Decisiveness as a skill. Treat the 70% rule as a muscle. By deciding before you feel ready, you train yourself to handle the ambiguity that defines the C-suite and successful entrepreneurship.

Decide when you have 70% of the information. The other 30% comes from action.

Reversible vs. irreversible decisions

Two-way doors. Most entrepreneurial decisions, especially your initial offer, are reversible. If the market does not respond, you can walk back through the door and adjust.

The tattoo fallacy. Aspiring entrepreneurs treat their first offer like a permanent tattoo. Treat it like a dry-erase marker instead. It is just a guess about what people want, based on validation, and it can change continuously and easily.

Lowering the stakes. By categorizing your offer as a test rather than a legacy, you remove the emotional weight that causes paralysis, and you can iterate with speed.

Treat your offer like a temporary patch, not a tattoo. You can change it many times after validation.

Fear audit using the Risk Matrix

Pull out your completed Risk Assessment Matrix (Doc 08). What is the real risk of testing this offer? Most fears are high-probability but low-impact. Let's calibrate.

Objective calibration. Use the matrix to move fear from your gut to the page. When you quantify a fear, you strip it of its emotional power and turn it into a manageable data point.

High probability, low impact. Most startup fears live in this quadrant. They are likely to happen (someone says no), but the actual impact on your life or career is negligible.

The risk of inaction. The most important part of the audit is measuring the risk of doing nothing. Compared to the certainty of staying stuck, the path forward becomes the only logical choice.

Writing a fear down usually makes it vanish. 76% of hospice patients said they regretted the things they never did.

"As a fighter pilot, hesitation kills. In business, hesitation just wastes time, but the principle is the same. You have enough information. Let's commit."

Offer Hypothesis Canvas review

This section delivered by Tom Cox

This is an important element in your overall business plan. Update it and create new versions as your hypothesis grows and shifts based on real-world contact. Below is a full worked example using the Executive Career Accelerator.

Worked example: Executive Career Accelerator offer canvas

Who (your ideal client)

Ideal client: Mid-career professionals age 35 to 55, currently a high-level individual contributor, manager, director, or VP, seeking the next level and ready to invest in a solution. They usually have a mortgage, children, and expenses that require them to earn more. They tend to be the primary breadwinner, and many want to retire their spouse. They are authentic, empathetic, and audacious.

Current situation: They work very hard in a role they don't love and want to move to the next level.

The trigger: They are motivated to earn more income and reduce the busy-work they do every day.

The problem

The one core problem we solve: We help mid-career professionals break past the hidden ceiling and move to the next level in their career, internally or externally.

The cost: Every month can cost $5,000 to $10,000. After a year that is $60,000 to $120,000 in lost compensation, plus the stress of working beneath their capability.

The outcome (dream state)

We turn mid-career professionals into aspiring executive leaders with real executive skills and mindsets, and train them to be seen as a leader internally and externally. Measured by how they show up, recruiters reaching out, raises, promotions, new higher-level roles, and clarity in their career roadmap.

The mechanism

The Whole Executive Framework: Foundations, Executive Upskilling, Network Activation, Interviewing & Negotiation, First 90 Days. It works because it is both holistic (start with understanding yourself as a human and a leader) and systemic (a step-by-step linear process from lost and frustrated to confident, decisive, clear, and visible).

Delivery model

Hybrid coaching (1:1, group, course). Engagement length 6 months. Client time 4 hours a week. Coach time 10 hours a week.

Pricing hypothesis

Initial price $10,000. Value of outcome to client is $100,000+ (a $20,000+ per year compensation increase over 5 years). Cost of not solving the problem is $100,000+ over 5 years plus years of frustration.

Anticipated objections

  • I can't afford this. You can't afford NOT to do this if you believe it will work.
  • This won't work for my industry. It works in every industry, all you do is follow the process.
  • I don't have the time. We help you manage your time like an executive so you prioritize the right things.
  • I need to think about it. What concern is coming up for you?
  • I need to check with my partner. We encourage you to talk it through and have them on board, it is a family investment in the family's income.
  • I had a bad experience with another coach. We have done this for 3 years with 250+ happy clients.

Transformation statement

I help mid-career professionals get unstuck and move to the executive level through the Whole Executive Framework, without feeling self-promotional.

Validation questions to test

  • Does this problem actually exist? (pain validation)
  • Is it painful enough that they'd pay to solve it? (urgency validation)
  • Does my language match how they describe the problem? (messaging validation)
  • Is the outcome I promise what they actually want? (outcome validation)
  • Does my price feel reasonable to them? (price validation)

Hot seats

Every participant gets 3 minutes to present Offer v1. The community and coaches get 3 minutes to give feedback on three questions: is this problem painful enough, is the outcome clear, and is the price justified?
