LinkedIn Mastery

Make your profile a landing page that attracts the right opportunities, and the 19-point checklist to get it there.

What you'll learn: some statistics to blow your mind; LinkedIn is Amazon and you are the product; the relationship between your executive resume and LinkedIn; how to use LinkedIn the right way to attract executive opportunities; and the Vertical Motion 19-point LinkedIn profile checklist.

Like it or not, LinkedIn is THE platform to find (and, more importantly, be found for) executive-level jobs. There are other platforms out there (Indeed, The Ladders, ExecuNet, etc.) but let's be clear here, LinkedIn is the first (and usually only) place recruiters and hiring managers search for talent. So let's learn how to maximize our ability to find, and be found on, this platform.

Part 1: LinkedIn by the numbers

  • LinkedIn has over 1.2 billion members in more than 200 countries worldwide. It's the largest professional network on the planet.
  • The platform sees 1.77 billion monthly visits.
  • In the US alone, 69% of users check LinkedIn daily.
  • Over 65 million business decision-makers; 10 million C-level executives; 180 million senior-level influencers.
  • 54% of US users earn more than $100K annually.
  • 72% of recruiters exclusively use LinkedIn when hiring new talent.
  • 87% of recruiters say LinkedIn is their most effective candidate screening tool.
  • 67% of recruiters believe that professionals hired through LinkedIn are of higher quality compared to those found through other methods.
  • 75% of people who recently changed jobs used LinkedIn to find new opportunities.
  • Profiles with multiple skill endorsements received 17x more views from recruiters.
And YOU are the product. LinkedIn is NOT (just) a social media platform. The real value of LinkedIn is that it is the world's largest and most easily-searched catalog of professional talent on the planet.

How LinkedIn makes money

LinkedIn's annual revenue reached $16.37B in 2024. If you use the platform for free, you are not the customer. You are the product. Specifically your data is the product, and more than $10B went to LinkedIn from recruiters and hiring managers trying to find you.

Part 3: LinkedIn vs. your executive resume

To be successful in your executive career, you will need both a solid LinkedIn profile and an executive resume. You need both: LinkedIn is how you get found; the resume matters during the interview process.

Part 4: How to use LinkedIn the right way

Having a great profile is important (see below for how to do that) but that is only the first step in using LinkedIn to attract hiring managers and executive recruiters to you. As we discussed in Deploying Your Brand, you need to become a thought leader on the platform to really attract the best opportunities.

Consistent, native, high-quality content

You should be sharing insights regularly on the platform. The biggest bang for your buck: comment on 1-2 posts in your field of interest/expertise 3-5 days a week, and publish 2 posts per week of around 200-400 words. Share insights, analyses of industry trends, leadership lessons from work and your broader life (remember your three pillars), and solutions to common executive challenges.

Consistency is really critical. The algorithm rewards profiles who post at least 2x/week every week. It's more important to post consistently than often, so if you can only sustain 1x/week, do that instead of posting 2x one week and nothing the next. Your posts should have a question in them toward the end that generates more meaningful comments, which the algorithm loves.

DO NOT post AI-generated slop. You will be penalized by the algorithm, and there is a coming backlash against obviously AI-generated content. You can use AI to help form your thoughts, help with grammar, and choose the right words, but the final words written should be yours alone, in your natural voice. We are building your brand, which needs to stand on its own with authenticity and trust.

Strategic networking

Connect strategically with executive recruiters specializing in your industry or function, as well as hiring managers at your target organizations. Also find relevant executive peers in your industry. You want to connect with as many people who might become an internal champion for you in their organization.

LinkedIn Groups

LinkedIn Groups are an underrated way to build connections and awareness fast. Join several groups in your target industry (or about your target organizations), and repost your content to those groups, while also engaging in the comments of other content posted there. Doing this will help move your content and your profile higher in the search rankings of recruiters looking for someone like you.

Share your core resume with recruiters

LinkedIn has a feature called "Resume Search" on the backend that recruiters can see. For this reason, it's a good idea to take your "base" resume and upload it to LinkedIn (in your job application settings). This allows them to find you based on keywords inside your resume even if they aren't listed on your LinkedIn profile. Also note, you can have literally DOZENS of resumes listed on your profile, it's best to have a resume for each type of role (and even for each specific role) to which you'll apply.

Other useful strategies

These strategies are increasingly more effective than the traditional ways of engaging on LinkedIn. Everything you do along these lines increases engagement, which in turn raises your profile in search rankings.

  • Show your thinking posts: beyond just stating your accomplishments, share your process and the thinking behind a successful initiative or solved problem.
  • AI fluency & skills integration: as AI becomes more integrated into recruitment, demonstrate your understanding and application of AI in executive environments, e.g. sharing how you've leveraged AI to improve efficiency, decision-making, or customer experience.
  • Micro-consulting: if you have a target organization in mind, create a "micro-consulting" piece, a short post or document identifying a challenge that organization might face, then offer a high-level solution without expecting anything in return.
  • AMA (Ask Me Anything) posts: once in a while write an AMA post and respond actively to questions. If you're a nuclear engineer wanting to move into a senior operations role, try posting "I am a nuclear engineer. AMA" and see what comes in.
  • Reverse engineering search parameters: think like a recruiter using the backend of LinkedIn. What Boolean search strings would a recruiter use to find their ideal candidate? Or use LinkedIn's Recruiter View (if you can access it) to see how your profile appears in searches.

Part 5: The VM 19-Point LinkedIn Profile Checklist

I. Strategic foundation & branding (the "Why me?" factor)

1. Professional photo: high-resolution, recent, professional headshot; smiling, approachable, confident expression; business professional attire; clean, uncluttered background (solid color or subtly blurred office); aligned with the gravitas of executive leadership you want to convey.

2. Compelling headline: beyond your title, craft a headline that immediately communicates your executive value proposition. Integrate 2-3 high-impact keywords relevant to target roles (e.g., "COO | Digital Transformation Leader | P&L Optimization"). What problem do you solve at an executive level? (e.g., "Driving Growth & Operational Excellence for Global Enterprises").

3. Dynamic "About" section (summary): a compelling narrative of your executive journey, accomplishments, and unique value. Quantify achievements with concrete numbers, percentages, and dollar figures (e.g., "Grew revenue by 30%," "Reduced operational costs by $XM"). Highlight leadership philosophy, strategic vision, cross-functional collaboration, and impact. Be future-focused: what executive challenges are you eager to tackle next? Include a subtle call to action (invite connections or discussions; consider a booking link).

4. Custom LinkedIn URL.

II. Experience & accomplishments (the "Show me the impact" factor)

5. Detailed experience section: go beyond job descriptions; for each executive role, focus on accomplishments and results, not just duties. Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, quantifiable Result). Use executive language ("spearheaded," "orchestrated," "transformed," "drove," "oversaw," "optimized," "created"), but don't go overboard (never use the word "synergy"). Highlight leadership scope: team size managed, budgets overseen, strategic initiatives led.

6. Quantify everything: revenue growth, cost savings, market share increase, efficiency gains, successful product launches, M&A deals closed. Numbers speak volumes to executive search professionals.

7. Share strategic initiatives you've taken on: detail large-scale projects, change management, digital transformations, global expansions, or turnarounds you've led.

III. Skills & endorsements (the "Can they do it?" factor)

8. Strategic skills selection: list 30-50 skills, prioritizing those highly sought after in executive roles (Strategic Planning, P&L Management, M&A, Digital Transformation, Board Governance, Global Operations, Executive Leadership, Change Management). Include industry-specific skills and soft skills (Leadership Development, Executive Coaching, Stakeholder Management, Visionary Leadership).

9. Endorsements: actively seek endorsements for your top skills from colleagues, direct reports, and superiors. Endorsements add credibility.

10. Recommendations: the gold standard, seek strong, specific recommendations from former bosses, board members, peers, and direct reports. Quality over quantity, a few powerful, detailed recommendations beat many generic ones. They should speak to your executive capabilities and impact. One give, one ask: offer to give recommendations in return.

IV. Education, licenses & accomplishments (credibility & depth)

11. Education: list degrees, certifications, and executive education programs; include prestigious institutions or relevant specializations.

12. Licenses & certifications: any professional licenses or executive-level certifications (e.g., PMP for certain roles, relevant board certifications).

13. Publications, patents, projects: if applicable, showcase thought leadership or significant contributions.

14. Honors & awards: any recognition for leadership, industry contributions, or professional excellence.

15. Volunteer experience: showcase board service for non-profits or significant leadership roles in community organizations. This demonstrates leadership beyond the workplace and commitment to impact.

V. Engagement & visibility (the "Get found" factor)

16. Thought leadership & content: curate and share articles, white papers, and industry news that align with executive insights. Publish original posts on LinkedIn regularly. Engage thoughtfully, comment intelligently on posts from industry leaders, news outlets, and potential connections.

17. Strategic networking: connect up and out, with executive recruiters, headhunters, hiring managers in target companies, board members, and fellow executives. Always send a personalized note with connection requests. Follow influencers and companies to stay abreast of developments and opportunities.

18. Join relevant groups: participate in executive-level groups, industry associations, and alumni networks for networking and visibility.

19. Be findable: ensure your profile is set to "public." Actively toggle "Open to Work" if you are openly seeking new roles, but use the more discreet "recruiters only" option if desired.
