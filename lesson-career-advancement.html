Advocating for Your Advancement

Get your boss and your boss's boss to see you as an executive candidate: define your value, craft your narrative, and build the relationships that promote you.

This class is about getting your boss and your boss's boss to see you as an executive candidate. We're going to define your value proposition, craft the narrative of your promotion, and build strategic relationships internally to make it happen.

The three parts of this lesson:

  1. Understanding Your Value Proposition (what makes you VP / C-Suite material?)
  2. Crafting Your Narrative (how to communicate your value effectively)
  3. Building Strategic Relationships (who needs to hear your narrative and how to reach them)

Understanding Your Value Proposition

Before you can advocate for yourself, you need to know what you're advocating for. It's not just about listing your accomplishments; it's about identifying the unique value you bring to the leadership table. Nobody cares what you have done. They care what you can do next.

The Leadership Gap

Let's talk about the difference between being a high-performing individual contributor and a strategic leader. Most of us got to where we are because we were good at doing things. To move up, we need to get good at leading people. As we've covered in this module, you need to hone these leadership competencies:

  • Vision & Strategy
  • Influence & Communication
  • Decision-Making
  • Handling Adversity & Uncertainty
  • Building High-Performing Teams

Self-Assessment

Let's run a quick self-assessment on your value proposition.

  • Strengths: Go beyond just job titles you've had. What are your core strengths? Are you great at problem-solving, relationship-building, innovative thinking, driving results, mentoring others, etc.?
  • Accomplishments: Don't just list tasks; quantify your results. Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to frame accomplishments. Focus on things that created impact to your organization, the industry, and the world.
  • Unique Value: What sets you apart? What problems can you solve that others can't? What perspective do you bring? This could be industry expertise, a specific skill set, a unique network, etc.
Exercise (5 minutes), Value Proposition Worksheet: List 3 core strengths. List 2 key accomplishments (using STAR). Write 1-2 sentences describing your unique value to the organization's future leadership needs.

Crafting Your Narrative

Your value proposition needs to be packaged into a compelling and consistent narrative that resonates with your target audience (your boss, senior leaders, etc.). Telling the story of how you help the organization as a strategic leader vs. a trusted team member is what will make the difference in your advancement path.

Know your audience

What are their priorities? What challenges are they facing? Your narrative should address their needs, not just your ambitions.

The elevator pitch

You need to have a clear and succinct message to tell when you have a few moments of opportunity with senior leaders. This is more than just an elevator pitch. It's the opener to a new conversation about your role in the organization. The structure:

  • Current Role & Key Contributions (briefly)
  • Aspiration (clearly state your desire for a leadership role, VP, C-Suite, etc.)
  • Value Proposition (connect your strengths and accomplishments to the organization's future needs and the audience's priorities)
  • Call to Action (subtle, e.g., "I'm eager to discuss how I can contribute at a higher level.")
Example: "As Senior Marketing Manager, I've consistently exceeded targets, leading the team that launched our most successful product last year. I'm passionate about driving growth and developing talent, and I'm seeking to take on a VP of Marketing role. My experience in [specific area] aligns perfectly with the company's expansion into [new market], and I'm confident I can lead the team to achieve significant results. I'd welcome the opportunity to discuss how my skills can contribute to our future success."

Consistent messaging across platforms

Your narrative should be consistent across your resume, LinkedIn profile, performance reviews, and conversations with organizational leaders. Say the same thing over and over again, that's how you establish a brand.

  • LinkedIn: Optimize your profile to highlight leadership potential (not just past accomplishments). Use keywords relevant to your desired roles.
  • Performance Reviews: Proactively frame your accomplishments in terms of leadership potential. Seek feedback on your leadership skills.

Storytelling

Probably the most important aspect is learning how to tell a story in such a way as to anchor your message in the minds of everyone who hears it. Tell your story in a way that is memorable and visual. For example, I talk often about how Leukemia really transformed my worldview and ultimately my entire career (for the better).

  • Use anecdotes and examples to bring your narrative to life
  • Focus on impact and results
  • Show, don't just tell

Talk about you in their future: What can you accomplish with more responsibility, more agency? What is the FINANCIAL ROI for putting you in a higher-level role? How will promoting you make each of them more successful?

Building Strategic Relationships

To move up in your organization, you need executive champions who are helping pull you up. Here's how to build those relationships.

Identify your executive champions

  • Your direct manager (obviously), but this person is not usually who promotes you.
  • Your manager's manager, this person is usually who is promoting you.
  • Other senior leaders in your department or related areas.
  • HR leaders involved in talent management and succession planning.
  • Mentors and sponsors (both internal and external).

Networking strategically

This isn't about collecting business cards. This is about building genuine relationships with other executives.

  • Informational Interviews: Reach out to people in roles you aspire to. Ask about their career paths, challenges, and advice.
  • Company Events: Don't just socialize; use these opportunities to really connect with key leaders. Get them to see the potential in you.
  • Cross-Functional Collaboration: Seek out opportunities to work with people from other departments. Demonstrate your leadership skills in these settings.

Mentorship and sponsorship

  • Mentor: someone who provides guidance and advice.
  • Sponsor: someone who actively advocates for your advancement within the organization. (This is crucial.)
  • How to find them: Identify individuals you admire and who have influence. Start by building a relationship; don't ask for sponsorship outright.

Communicating with your boss

  • Regularly discuss your career goals and aspirations with your direct supervisor. Don't hide the fact you want to grow your career.
  • Seek feedback on your performance and areas for development.
  • Proactively offer solutions to challenges facing the team or organization.
  • Don't be afraid to ask for opportunities to demonstrate your leadership potential (e.g., leading a project, presenting to senior leadership).
  • Find out about internal leadership training opportunities, or new initiatives you can get engaged in.

The Key Influencer Diagram

The Key Influencer Diagram is a tool built on how you would triangulate a customer organization in business development. You need to know who the Key Decision Maker (KDM) is, but also who the Approver is, as well as all of the influencers. Some influencers are more influential than others. Some are positive towards you, some neutral, some negative, and some don't know you. Watch the video on the tool and fill in your own Key Influencer Diagram at your organization.
