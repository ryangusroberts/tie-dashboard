Why we need a career roadmap

Like every transformation journey, you have to start somewhere, and wind up somewhere else. It's vitally important to define your goals, otherwise you won't know what direction to head. This roadmap is built on the understanding that becoming an executive is not just about climbing the ladder; it's about a fundamental shift in mindset, skills, and strategic impact. You need to know who you need to be in order to become the version of yourself that attracts and successfully implements the future you want. So with that, let's dive in.

Wherever your Income Dial points, this roadmap works. Your destination might be a bigger role (Career), a bridge through a transition (Bridge), or your own practice (Build). The exercise is the same: name where you're going, then work backward.

II. Defining your primary career goal

We've established who you are. We've also established where you are. So we have your starting point. Now we need to establish where you are going. This is where we will define your Primary Career Goal. This can be a goal for your next position, or it can also be your penultimate goal. For example, if you are a Senior Director in a small business but your primary goal is to become the CEO of a Fortune 500 company, that's not likely to happen right away. I suggest focusing on where you want to go next, but you can use this however you wish.

The point is to understand that there is a place and time at which you will have achieved the goal you are setting today. Your goals can and will change over time, but imagine where you want to be when you would declare success in this moment in your life.

Primary career goal

To define your Primary Career Goal, you need the following:

  • Title, or specific role, that you are aiming to achieve.
  • Industry, types of organizations, or a specific organization in which you want to work.
  • Specific focus / vertical in that organization (sales, operations, finance, etc.).

My Primary Career Goal is to become [title / specific role] in [industry] working at [specific organization or type of organization], so I can [achievement or focus].

Here are a few examples to consider:

  • My Primary Career Goal at one point was to become a Vice President of Innovation, or VP Product Development, or VP R&D, in the Space & Satellite industry, specifically with SpaceX, Lockheed Martin, York Space Systems or a similar company, so I can build the technology that will make humans a space-faring race.
  • My Primary Career Goal is to become the CEO of Vertical Motion in the executive coaching industry, so I can work with aspiring leaders and guide them to become authentic, empathetic and audacious executives.

Personal legacy

Here you want to zoom out and consider what you want to accomplish in the rest of your career. Before you retire, or move on to a life where you're not working as much, what long-term goals would you like to have accomplished? These can be intrinsic, extrinsic, global or personal.

III. Strategic skill gap analysis

Next, let's consider what skills you lack that would be required to move to your primary goal. You may not need to master all of these skills, some are learned along the way and once you're in the role. There's a list of strategic skills provided (though it is not exhaustive). Choose 4-5 skills you'd like to focus on next, or include some skills that aren't on the list. As you grow through this program and your own efforts, come back to this list and check off what you accomplish.

IV. Putting it all together

Finally we come to the core of this exercise: the roadmap itself. Let's summarize who you are, where you are, where you want your career to go, and what you must do next to get there. This will help you understand what skills you need to develop, what your strengths and weaknesses are, and what you actually need to do to move forward.
