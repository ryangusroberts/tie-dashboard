90-day growth plan and scale architecture

This training provided by Ryan Roberts

The 90-day plan: three targets

A 90-day plan is not a wish list. It's three concrete targets with specific milestones attached to each. Revenue target: what dollar amount do you need to hit in 90 days? Not "more revenue," a number, tied to your Freedom Number from pre-work. Client target: how many active paying clients does that require? Work backward from your pricing (if your offer is $3K/month and your target is $15K, the math is simple). System target: what one system needs to be built or improved to support those clients without you being the bottleneck?

Most founders set revenue goals and skip the system goal. That's how you hit the number once and can't sustain it.

Ryan: "When I built the 90-day plan for my defense company, I didn't start with revenue. I started with: what breaks if we double? Then I worked backward to the revenue number the systems could actually support. Plan for what the infrastructure can hold, not what your ambition wants."

Doc: 90-Day Growth Plan. Three targets (revenue, clients, systems), milestones for each at 30, 60, and 90 days. Complete this live, don't leave the call with blanks.

Scale levers: the 80/20

You've been doing a lot of things over the past 8 weeks. Not all of them matter equally. The question: if you could only do THREE things next quarter, which three would generate the most revenue? This is Pareto applied to your business. Most founders spread effort evenly across 10 activities; the ones who grow fastest concentrate on the 2 to 3 that actually produce clients.

Common activityUsually a lever?
Direct outreach to ideal prospectsYes
Posting content on LinkedInDepends on traction
Refining your websiteNo, at this stage
Delivery to current clientsYes (retention = revenue)
Building new offersNo, too early
Networking eventsDepends on conversion
Referral asks after client winsYes

The answer is almost always outreach, delivery, referrals. Everything else is a multiplier on those three, not a replacement for them.

Ryan: "On the bombing range, there's a concept called 'weapons employment zone,' the specific conditions where your weapon is effective. Outside that zone, you're wasting ordnance. Your scale levers are your employment zone. Work inside them. Stop lobbing effort at things outside the zone."

Doc: Scale Lever Analysis. Identify your top 3 levers. What are you doubling down on? What are you stopping? Write both lists.

Risk assessment for growth

This is not the fear exercise from the start. That was about starting; this is about growing. Different risks at this stage: Capacity risk, you land more clients than you can serve well, delivery quality and retention drop, reputation suffers. Single-channel risk, all your clients come from one source and if that channel dries up, pipeline goes to zero. Revenue concentration risk, one client represents more than 40% of your income and if they leave you're in trouble. Burnout risk, you're doing everything yourself and the pace isn't sustainable past 90 days. For each risk: what's the probability, what's the impact, what's your mitigation plan?

Ryan: "In aviation, we plan for contingencies before we take off, not when the engine is on fire. Same principle. You map the risks now, when things are going well, so you have a plan before you need one."

Doc: Risk Assessment for Growth. Map your top 3 growth risks with probability, impact, and mitigation. This is your contingency plan for the next 90 days.

Long-term vision and identity consolidation

This training provided by Tom Cox

Identity consolidation: who you've become

Eight weeks ago, most of you had an idea and a set of fears. Now many of you have clients, a delivery system, an acquisition process, a KPI dashboard, and a 90-day plan. You're fundamentally a different person than you were 8 weeks ago.

Week 1Week 8
"I'm thinking about starting a business""I run a business"
"I don't know if anyone would pay for this""I have paying clients"
"I'm not sure what to charge""I have a pricing model backed by data"
"I don't know how to find clients""I have an acquisition SOP"
"I'm afraid of rejection""I've been rejected and I'm still here"

Tom: "I've been CEO three times and a CPO before that. Every time I stepped into a bigger role, the hardest part wasn't the skills, it was believing I belonged in the seat. You've earned this seat. The evidence is in every document you've completed and every client you've served. Don't just skip past that."

Long-term vision: beyond 90 days

The 90-day plan is tactical. The vision worksheet is directional. 12-month vision: what does the business look like in a year, in revenue, client count, team, and lifestyle? Be specific. 3-year vision: what's the model? Solo practitioner, a small team, productized, licensing your methodology? What does your week look like? 10-year vision: what's the legacy? What did this business make possible in your life, and who did you become?

The purpose is alignment. Your 90-day plan should be a step toward your 12-month vision, which should be a step toward your 3-year. If the short-term plan doesn't connect to the long-term direction, something needs to change now, not later.

Tom: "Most people plan in circles, busy without direction. The founders who build something lasting are the ones who can connect today's action to a 10-year outcome. You don't need to get the 10-year answer right. You just need to have an answer."

Doc: Long-Term Vision Worksheet. 12-month, 3-year, 10-year. Complete all three, then check: does your 90-day plan point toward the 12-month vision?

Program completion: the final audit

Look back at everything you built over 8 weeks: pre-work foundation (who you are, what you know, what you need), your starting hypothesis, Offer v1, validation data, a sales engine, real delivery, brand and testimonials, scaling infrastructure, an operating system, and direction. Don't think of the Build track as a course you completed. Think of it as the paradigm change you've made in your life.

Doc: Program Completion Checklist. Go through every document, verify completion, flag anything incomplete. You have the Implementation Phase to finish it, but name it now.

Celebration

This is not optional. Do not skip this segment. Round 1, wins board: every participant shares their biggest win, a specific outcome (first client, first revenue, a delivery system, a referral, a named mindset shift), not "I learned a lot." Round 2, peer acknowledgment: each person names one other participant who helped, challenged, or inspired them. Round 3, testimonials: if you haven't submitted one yet, this is the ask.

"If this program made a difference for you, a 2-minute written or video testimonial helps the next person make the decision you made 8 weeks ago. Would you be willing to share your experience?"

What comes next: Implementation Phase

The 8-week sprint is over. The Implementation Phase begins now, carrying you through months 3 to 12. What stays the same: weekly 60-minute Advanced Topics calls, community access (#wins, #questions, #accountability), and the peer network. What changes: calls shift from foundational curriculum to advanced standalone topics designed for mixed-experience rooms, and hot seats continue with real deals and decisions. What's expected: run your KPI dashboard every Monday, follow your CEO calendar, execute on your 90-day plan, and show up to calls and contribute.

"The implementation sprint gave you the systems. The Implementation Phase is where you master them. The difference between people who build a real business and people who had a good 8 weeks is what happens in the next 90 days."
