Validation conversations recap

From Kickoff we charged you with at least 5 validation conversations. These are not sales calls. They are conversations with people who have the pain you are trying to solve, so you can decide how best to solve it.

Validation conversations are the best way to ensure that what you sell is something someone actually wants to buy.

Two documents help you: the Validation Conversation Guide (to set up and frame the conversation, and select your first 5 people) and the Conversation Debrief (to collect your thoughts afterward). Use AI to help gather your notes, then step back to a high-level view to spot trends and themes.

Listening for truth

This training provided by Ryan Roberts

Confirmation bias warning. Your brain wants to believe your offer is good. It will filter data to confirm what you already believe. Be ruthless with yourself.

The most dangerous words in validation: "They seemed interested." Polite interest is not signal.

Signal vs. noise

Noise (don't be fooled)Signal (pay attention)
"That sounds interesting""How do I sign up?"
"Let me think about it""When can we start?"
"Send me more information""What's the investment?"
Enthusiasm from people who can't buyUrgency from people who can buy

PMF signals vs. false signals

True PMF signals: they described the pain with emotion before you mentioned it; they have already tried to solve it (spent money, time, effort); they asked "How do I work with you?" without prompting; they referred you to someone else with the problem.

False signals: "that sounds interesting" (polite dismissal); "let me think about it" (no urgency); "send me more information" (procrastination); enthusiasm from people who can't buy (wrong ICP).

Red flags to watch for

  • You had to convince them the problem existed
  • They couldn't articulate the pain without prompting
  • They've never tried to solve this before
  • They flinched at any price point
  • They kept changing the subject
If you have to convince people the problem exists, that's a red flag.

"In the military we called this ground truth: what's actually happening vs. what the briefing said would happen. Your conversations are ground truth. Don't spin the data to make yourself feel better."

Pattern recognition

When you have done enough validation conversations, patterns emerge, and you start to understand who you can best serve and how. Watch for when multiple prospects mention a similar situation or challenge, use similar language, or react similarly to your pricing. If no one flinched at your price, you might be undercharging. If no one felt it was realistic, you might not have found the real pain.

Ideal Client Profile (ICP) recap

This training provided by Tom Cox

Your ICP can be described in concentric rings from bullseye to edge case. ICP-1 is a perfect match: they need exactly what you do, are aligned culturally, and agree on the value. ICP-2 is an okay match who may need more convincing. ICP-3 is not a match: no pain, different mindset, don't see your value. The more people you talk to, the sharper your ICP-1 becomes.

Beware of going too broad in defining your ICP-1. The more targeted you are, the easier it is for those few customers to invest in you.

Example of a hyper-targeted offer: Pat, a fat-loss specialist on Instagram, defined his ICP-1 as nurses, who are Asian, in the greater Los Angeles area, over 40. He has more than 1.1M followers. He also works with ICP-2 and ICP-3 clients, but his ICP-1 is dialed in better than anyone.

Offer iteration

It takes multiple iterations to reach a product or service your ideal client will pay for. Two things move constantly in the early phase: the offer, and the ICP-1. Not only will what you offer change, but who you offer it to will change as well.

Don't be afraid to constantly tweak your offer, or even rethink it entirely. This process takes time. Don't force it.

When to iterate vs. pivot

Iterating is continuous tweaks to your core offer or ICP-1: niching down, trying different niches, a slightly different service, testing different prices. Pivoting is a significant shift away from your current hypothesis and ICP-1: a different market, an unrelated problem, or a different delivery mode (consulting to SaaS, for example).

DecisionWhen to use it
ProceedMultiple prospects showed genuine buying signals
IterateRight problem, right ICP, but mechanism or messaging needs work
PivotWrong problem (no urgency), wrong ICP (can't pay), or the solution doesn't fit

Hot seats: debrief and decisions

Each person shares their top insights and their go/no-go decision (3 to 4 minutes each): one minute on "my number one insight was...", one minute on "my PMF score is X, my decision is go, iterate, or pivot", then 1 to 2 minutes of coach calibration. Questions to consider: what one thing would you change about your offer based on what you heard? Did anyone explicitly say they'd pay, and who? If you had to sell this next week, are you ready?
