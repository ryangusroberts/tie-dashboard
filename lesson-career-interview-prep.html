What you'll learn: general interview preparation skills, virtual interview preparation skills, in-person interview preparation skills, and a pre-interview checklist.

Now that we have our mindset right about interviewing, let's dive into how to absolutely crush that interview: the preparation phase.

Getting prepared for the interview

Practice

Before the interview, if you are not confident that you will do well because of lingering anxiety, it's best to have done a practice interview. You can work with a friend or a career coach on a mock interview if you feel like you need that practice.

Research the organization

Know their history: their founding story, how they grew to where they are now, the current leadership team, and any connections you might have either with the organization directly or any other organizations they work closely with (especially customers of theirs). Make sure you know their products, their market, customers, regulatory environment, etc.

Research their culture

You can find a lot out about a company on their own website in the About Us, Mission & Values, and Culture pages. Additionally, Glassdoor will usually have some context from former and current employees. Take the egregious complaints you see on there with a grain of salt, most people who take the time to leave a comment on Glassdoor have an axe to grind. You can also search the company on social media (LinkedIn especially) and in industry trade magazines. Finally it doesn't hurt to talk to former employees for their insight on the organization.

Prepare thoughtful questions for the interview

Come with a list of questions that are important for YOU to understand about the role, the organization, etc. The more specific your questions the better both you and the interviewer will feel about you fitting the role.

Have your resume / CV, cover letter, and position description available

You want to have all of the documentation about this role, the organization, and yourself that you've shared with them available in case you need to reference them.

For virtual interviews, it's best to print some of this out, but you can also have a second monitor with the information readily available to look at while you keep the interviewers on full-screen on your main monitor. If you are using one screen, it's really best to have these documents physically available so you don't have to constantly swap screens away from the interviewers and risk the video app freezing on you.

When going to a physical interview, bring multiple printed copies of your resume or CV with you in a professional-looking padfolio or folder. About 7-10 copies is plenty. Also bring a copy of the position description. I usually bring 2-3 copies because invariably an interviewer will not have one available for him/herself.

Presentation

This is up to you and very dependent on the role and the culture of the organization, but I really like having a presentation available when I go to interviews. The presentation depends heavily on who you are, what kind of role you're seeking, and what your background is. But you can do some publicly-available research on the organization and share your thoughts and ideas about possible growth or cost-savings that come to mind for you even without any internal context. The point is to showcase your presentation skills, not necessarily what the content is about.

Virtual interview preparation

Know the platforms

You might want to spend time downloading and configuring multiple videoconferencing apps that you might wind up using in interviews, such as Zoom, WebEx, Microsoft Teams, Amazon Chime, and Google Hangouts.

Trust your computer

Use a reliable device with up-to-date software and operating system. If you are using a new video conferencing app for the first time on the interview, test it out ahead of time to make sure everything works correctly on your machine.

Check your internet connection

From your computer go to fast.com and see what your connection speed is. If it's 5 MBPS or greater consistently for both UPLOAD and DOWNLOAD, you should be fine for the interview, as most video conference apps use about 2-3 MBPS for a high-quality connection.

Have a backup internet connection available

Most of us can use our smartphones as a hotspot. Make sure you test that process out WELL BEFORE the interview.

In-person interview preparation

Travel

Sometimes in-person interviews involve traveling to another city for the interview. Typically the company interviewing you will pay for your travel. Sometimes they will book your travel. Sometimes they will reimburse you for your expenses while you book the travel. Make sure you book everything with enough time to deal with travel challenges such as flight delays and empty rental car lots. And book a nice hotel with a comfortable bed. Consider investing in an eye mask and ear plugs in case there is a ton of noise outside of your hotel.

I once stayed at an AirBnB in downtown Sydney, Australia. It was a phenomenal rate and an amazing flat, right up until we realized that they were doing construction on the street down below overnight. The construction team started jackhammering at 10PM and didn't stop until 4AM every single night we were there.

Attire

Dress appropriately for the industry. It's generally better to be slightly overdressed than underdressed. My rule of thumb is to dress one level above what you would expect to wear on a normal day in the office for this company's culture. If you are traveling, bring two options just in case something happens to the first. And make sure everything fits and is clean before you pack it.

Resumes, CVs, position description

When going to a physical interview, bring multiple printed copies of your resume or CV with you in a professional-looking padfolio or folder. About 7-10 copies is plenty. Also bring a copy of the position description. I usually bring 2-3 copies because invariably an interviewer will not have one available for him/herself.

Logistics

Know the location of the company, where to park, what building / office to go to, and consider doing a trial run if possible. I like to drive to the location and figure things out about an hour before the interview. If I have enough time I'll head over to a coffee shop or restaurant to just sit in a booth and collect my thoughts.

Punctuality

Aim to arrive 10-15 minutes before the interview is scheduled to start. This will allow time to sign in, get badged, and to coordinate internal processes like contacting an escort or setting up a conference room.

"The handshake"

Offer a firm but not crushing handshake. I could talk for hours about this nuance, but please ignore and immediately delete anything in your brain that you heard about asserting dominance through a handshake. If you crush my hand in a handshake, or, worse, pull some stupid power move like yanking my arm towards you, I will assume you are either a sociopath or an idiot and the interview will be over before it started.

Only slightly less awful than the supposed manhood trial crushing handshake is the "dead fish" handshake. I will never forget the incredibly uncomfortable handshake I had with someone in December of 2002 who offered his hand and let it just 'be' there for the shake, like I was holding a dead fish in my hand for a moment.

When you shake someone's hand, grasp their hand as if you are taking a utensil from them, shake up and down slightly once or twice, and release. Be slightly more gentle with women, slightly more firm with very large men. Aim to have a handshake that exudes confidence and comfort, not fear.
