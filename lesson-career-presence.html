What is Executive Presence?

First of all, let's define what Executive Presence actually is.

Think of executive presence as the way you carry yourself, the way you communicate, and the way you interact with others. Executive presence is partially tied to your role or title (CEO commands more presence than assistant janitor), but it's tied way more to how you are perceived by others in terms of your competence, confidence, and ability to have a positive impact on your environment.

There are 3 Key Factors that make up Executive Presence:

  • Gravitas, the weight of your personality in a space, and the confidence you exude
  • Communication Skill, the ability to inspire confidence and trust in your environment
  • Appearance, how you show up to those around you

76% of participants indicated that the leader's confidence is a key component to executive presence. If you don't feel confident in your ability as a leader, your team will absolutely notice that.

Things that Enhance Executive Presence

Here are some things you can practice to increase your level of executive presence in just about any situation.

  • Articulate your vision and ideas clearly.
  • There is great power in brevity. If you want to amplify your message, simplify it.
  • Never waste a crisis. Demonstrate an ability to operate efficiently in high-stress situations.
  • Know thyself, know your strengths and weaknesses as a leader and be candid about both.
  • Be aware of how others see you and adjust accordingly (you can ask for 360 feedback).
  • Exude calmness in most situations, especially high-stakes / high-pressure ones.
  • Pay attention to your posture, gestures, and facial expressions.
  • Keep a positive attitude. Your optimism will inspire and motivate others.

Things that Destroy Executive Presence

And likewise here are some things you should stop doing altogether, or try to reduce as much as possible in your behaviors, as you will degrade your presence.

  • Avoid coming across as overly confident or arrogant. It's important to strike a balance between confidence and humility.
  • Maintain professionalism in your interactions. Unprofessional behavior, such as gossiping, using inappropriate language, or displaying a lack of respect or a lack of tolerance will degrade how people see you.
  • Avoid ineffective communication practices, such as interrupting others, dominating conversations, or failing to listen actively. Strive for clear and concise communications that promotes understanding and collaboration.
  • Take responsibility for your actions and decisions. Avoid making excuses or shifting blame to others. Demonstrating accountability builds trust and credibility.
  • Strive to be consistent in your behavior, values, and decision-making. Inconsistency can create confusion and erode trust.
  • Be open to new ideas, perspectives, and feedback. Avoid being rigid or dismissive of alternative viewpoints. Embrace a growth mindset and actively seek opportunities for learning and personal growth.
  • Avoid negativity and complaining. It will destroy morale and diminish your leadership presence. Instead, focus on finding solutions and maintaining a positive outlook.
  • Cultivate empathy and understanding towards others. Avoid dismissing or disregarding others' feelings or perspectives. Show genuine interest and support for the well-being of your team members.
  • Be mindful of your body language and non-verbal cues. Avoid crossing your arms, avoiding eye contact, or displaying defensive, offensive, or dismissive gestures. Project openness and engagement through your body language.
  • Avoid being resistant to change or clinging to outdated methods. Embrace innovation and demonstrate a willingness to adapt to new situations and challenges.

Do You Have EP?

Ask yourself these questions and reflect on your honest answers to these.

  • Do people take you seriously?
  • Do people value your opinion?
  • Do people take you at your word?
  • Do people second-guess or argue with your decisions?
  • Do you inspire confidence and boost morale in your team?
  • Do you actively listen to others and make them feel heard and valued?
  • How well do you handle pressure and maintain composure in demanding or high-stakes situations?
  • Do you demonstrate adaptability and flexibility in the face of change or unexpected circumstances?
  • Do you project professionalism in your behavior, appearance, and overall conduct?
  • Do you demonstrate emotional intelligence by understanding and managing your own emotions, as well as empathizing with others?
