First, the mental model: Cowork vs Claude Code

Think of Cowork as your AI employee and Claude Code as your AI engineer. You'll live in Cowork and only reach for Code when you're building an actual software tool. Both are the same Claude underneath. What differs is the workspace and what it can build.

What it isAn AI teammate that works your files, apps, and browser on your computerAn AI developer that writes and runs real code
Best forRunning the business day to day: docs, dashboards, trackers, research, admin, simple agentsBuilding custom software: apps, integrations, automations with real logic

Rule of thumb: Start every task in Cowork. Move to Claude Code only when you need a durable, reusable tool with real logic that you'll run again and again, and even then, Claude writes the code, not you. Most solopreneurs run their first year almost entirely in Cowork.

Build it: follow along in Cowork

Do each step in order. Copy the prompt, run it, and adjust by conversation ("make it simpler," "add a column") until it's right.

  1. Load your knowledge. Connect Cowork to your course folder, then prove it worked.
    Here is my course folder and my completed worksheets. Read them and tell me: my offer, my current stage, and my top 3 gaps.
  2. Build your dashboard. Turn the course into a home base you reopen anytime.
    Build me a one-page business dashboard I can reopen and update. Sections: my stage progress, this week's KPIs, active deals, and my top 3 tasks.
  3. Build your KPI tracker.
    Create a weekly KPI tracker for a solo founder: validation conversations, sales calls booked, offers made, revenue, and LinkedIn activity. Make it a table I update by telling you the numbers.
  4. Build your dealflow tracker.
    Create a dealflow tracker: each prospect with stage (Lead, Conversation, Proposal, Won or Lost), value, next action, and date. Let me add deals just by chatting.
  5. Add a light cash view.
    Add a simple cash view: monthly revenue, expenses, and runway in months.
  6. Update by conversation. This is the magic moment. You never touch a spreadsheet again.
    Add a deal: Acme Co, proposal stage, $6k, follow up Friday. Also, this week I had 5 validation calls and made 2 offers, update my KPIs.
  7. Hire your first agent. Turn a weekly chore into an automation.
    Every Monday at 7am, review my trackers and send me a digest: last week's KPIs, which deals moved, and the 3 things that need me this week.
  8. Know where Claude Code comes in (nothing to build yet). When you outgrow this, say you want a calculator your clients use, or your Stripe revenue flowing in automatically, that's a Claude Code job. Same conversation, but Claude builds real software you own. You're not there yet, and that's the point.
End state: a working dashboard, three live trackers, and one agent, all built by talking.

Give your AI the full picture of you

The more your AI knows about you, the better it coaches you, on the course, your career, and your business. Beyond your course files, load two more things:

  • Your LDNA assessment. Share your results and ask Claude to name your complementary archetypes and your blind spots, then have it deliberately play those roles for you, covering the gaps a co-founder normally would.
  • Your LinkedIn. Give Claude your profile so it knows your background, positioning, and network.

Two ways to load them: turn on Claude in Chrome (or let Claude drive your computer) and point it at the page, or just copy and paste. Either works. Once Claude has your course work, your LDNA, and your LinkedIn, it stops being a generic assistant and becomes a coach that actually knows you, sharpening every decision in the program, your career, and your business.

Your operating lens: Eliminate, Automate, Delegate

From now on, run every recurring task through one question, in this order:

  1. Eliminate. Does this need doing at all? The cheapest task is the one you kill.
  2. Automate. If it must happen and it's repeatable, hand it to an agent or scheduled task. No human touch.
  3. Delegate. If it needs judgment, delegate it: first to AI with your review, later to a person.

Example, a weekly client update: Eliminate (does it need to be weekly, or would monthly do?), then Automate (an agent drafts it from your trackers every Friday), then Delegate (you glance, approve, send). One chore, cut down and mostly handed off.

Make it a weekly habit: pick one recurring task and run it through the three questions. Your calendar and your course processes are the hunting ground.

Your staff of agents: what to hand off

You're building a staff of agents, each one a job title AI holds. Every recurring task that survives the Eliminate/Automate/Delegate lens becomes a role on your org chart. There are three ways to set each one up:

  • One-off: ask in Cowork the moment you need it, "research this prospect before my 2pm."
  • Scheduled task: recurring work that runs on a clock without you, the Monday metrics digest.
  • Saved agent: a reusable specialist you invoke on demand, your "proposal writer" or "post drafter."
FunctionJobs you can hand to an agentTypical setup

MarketingDraft posts from your wins, turn one idea into a week of content, a content calendarSaved agent + scheduled

Finance & adminWeekly KPI and cash digest, invoice reminders, expense sorting, inbox triageScheduled

The principle: hire the agent that removes your biggest weekly bottleneck first, then add one every week or two. Your org chart is a list of agents. You only add a human when one is genuinely cheaper or better than the agent doing that job.

A few ground rules

  • Keep yourself in the loop for money and sending, review anything before it goes to a client or moves funds.
  • Own your accounts and data, and keep a copy of your key trackers.
  • Never paste passwords or secrets into a prompt.

Keep going

Your operating system grows with you:

  • Now (solo): Cowork runs your dashboard, trackers, and a couple of agents. Almost no code.
  • Traction: more agents, lead follow-up, content repurposing, invoice reminders, and maybe a first small Claude Code tool.
  • Scaling: Claude Code integrations (Stripe, a CRM) and client-facing tools; AI holds the ops so your first hires do only higher-value work.
Add an agent before you add a person. Hire only for the judgment and relationships AI can't hold, that's how you stay free while the business grows. One habit to carry through the whole program: after every worksheet you complete, tell Claude "add this to my dashboard." By the end, your operating system runs your company.
