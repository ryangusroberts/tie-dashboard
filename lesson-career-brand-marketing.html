The 2020s hiring process in a nutshell

The standard hiring process is awful. The reality is that less than 1 in 10 of all resumes submitted to companies through online portals are ever even seen by a hiring manager. Estimates suggest that 75% of resumes are rejected by AI or ATS and never seen by any humans.

The Applicant Tracking Software, or ATS, does its best to sort applicants, but the reality is they are rarely configured correctly, and hiring managers complain all the time about the lack of qualified talent while qualified, talented candidates are rejected by the system. The online world is littered with examples of experts being rejected for jobs they are more than qualified to do. One of the craziest examples was of the software engineer who created the FastAPI web framework being deemed unqualified to work on FastAPI.

What's even more insidious is the fact that most HR recruiters and independent executive recruiters will look on LinkedIn before looking at the resumes that come into their ATS. They don't want to spend time looking at resumes and discerning if someone has relevant experience. They'd rather set up filters and force candidates to adapt to show them what they want to see. That's why recruiters will spend HOURS on LinkedIn cold-emailing and messaging potential candidates who "have the right experience" instead of looking at their own candidate pool.

The importance of brand

I want you to forget what you were taught to get to this point in your career. To go to the next level, the EXECUTIVE level, you need to play a different game. What got you here won't get you there. At this level, you have to be FOUND for your next role vs. finding your next role. So how do we do that? Brand.

Brand is what people say about you when you're not in the room. In the 90s we used to call this "cred" or credibility. Your brand is what does the heavy lifting for you when you're not there to make the case for yourself in person. It's a numbers game. You can only meet and talk to so many people in person. Establishing your brand online is how people find you who need what you do and want people like you in their culture.

The CEO of You, Inc.

Imagine you are the CEO of a business. You have all the usual departments: Operations, Finance, Sales, Marketing, etc.

Operations is where you spend most of your time, focusing on doing your job well. Sales is the interview process, where Marketing is getting people to consider you in the first place for a new role.

Imagine if a real business had just one customer who pays them 100% of their revenue every two weeks. And imagine they only staffed their operations department. There are no ongoing sales and marketing activities. Suddenly, that one customer decides to "go in a different direction" and their services are no longer needed. That company just lost 100% of its revenue, and it has no other customers lined up to replace that income. How long do you think the CEO would last in that situation? The board would be furious that the CEO didn't prepare for the very likely situation they would lose their customer (job).

This is the most important concept of this entire module. Executive-level roles are more scarce than professional-level roles. They have a "longer sales cycle," meaning they take longer to cultivate and turn into a real opportunity. So if you lose your job at this level, or want to move to a higher level role, you have to expect it will take longer to be found for that role. This means that if you lose your job you will likely need a few months to find your next one, unless you're willing to settle for something that's easy to find and probably beneath you. And the way we do that is to always be promoting yourself as a brand that attracts the organizations you feel most aligned with.

LinkedIn

Think about how companies market their products. They "advertise" them in places they think they will find their customers. Beer and truck commercials dominate NFL broadcasts because, well, people who like beer and trucks watch NFL games. We're not going to run commercials for ourselves on television. Instead, we're going to go where our target customers go, LinkedIn.

The professional social media network

LinkedIn is a social media platform. But it's less like Facebook and more like Amazon. And you are the product. Think about it. Who pays for LinkedIn? Sure, some of us pay for premium, but that makes up less than 20% of LinkedIn's revenue. The rest comes from hiring managers and executive recruiters who want access to the world's largest database of professional and executive talent in the world. Some recruiters pay up to $15K/mo per seat for their access to YOUR information.

Brand yourself like an Amazon product

Go to Amazon and search for something, anything. Let's say you are in the market for a new backpack. You type in "backpack" and you are presented with what seems like a never-ending list of options. But the reality is you look at the top results more closely than the lower results. And you pay most attention to the ones with videos of the product, several pictures with detailed explanations of how it works, and lots of positive reviews. In order to buy something on Amazon, you need to know, like, and trust the product. That Amazon product profile has some heavy lifting to do to get you there.

Look at your current LinkedIn profile

Now go to LinkedIn and imagine you are the hiring manager who is looking for someone JUST LIKE YOU. They want YOU because of what you do, what you know, your experience, your personality, your location, your cultural fit, your credentials and bona fides, and so on. Imagine you are a 100% fit for what they want to find. But put yourself in their place and look at your own profile. If your profile doesn't make it obvious, they won't know you are a perfect match.

The connection between LinkedIn & your resume

We were taught earlier in our career that our resume is critically important to finding and landing a job. This is partially true today. Both are necessary, but one is useless without the other. You could post your resume on LinkedIn, or even your own website. But without advertising, who will read it? The reality is the resume is one of the LAST pieces of information a hiring manager will gather about you, not the FIRST.

This is why you should be spending 90% of your energy on LinkedIn and 10% on the resume. Your LinkedIn profile is CRITICAL to being found, whereas the resume will be important during the interview process.

Keeping the marketing department staffed

Now that you have seen this training, you should know that from this day forward in your career you MUST staff your marketing department. You need to start thinking like an executive, specifically as the CEO of You, Inc. And that means that you will need to allocate time, energy, and resources to your brand. Every. Single. Week. Even when you love your current job, and have no interest in going elsewhere.

In the rest of this module, we will show you how to build your Personal Executive Brand, how to deploy that brand, and how to leverage it to bring you the opportunities you are looking for. But right now, you need to commit to yourself that you will invest time and energy into marketing and promoting yourself. The free ride is over, it ends at about the senior manager level. Above that, you have to lift yourself into the awareness of more people looking for solutions to their problems.
