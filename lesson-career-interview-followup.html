Follow-up protocol

When interviewing, email is the preferred method of communication until the interviewer, recruiter, or hiring manager contacts you in a different manner. You may also use LinkedIn Messenger if that's where you first spoke with one another, but email is king.

Know when to cut bait, and don't lock in on a single opportunity. While you're waiting to hear from your dream job, you should be out there looking for opportunities and offer letters that you can use as leverage in your negotiations.

Remember: in detachment, I am free.

Immediate follow-up: thank-you note (within 24 hours)

Method: an email is typically the most appropriate method for this initial follow-up.

Content: express gratitude for the opportunity to interview and reiterate your enthusiasm for the role. Reference specific topics discussed during the interview to make the note personalized.

Example: "Thank you for discussing [specific project/role challenge] with me. It gave me deeper insights into the role and reinforced my keen interest in contributing to [Company's Name] in the capacity of [Job Title]."

Secondary follow-up: reinforce interest (1 week after the interview if no response)

Method: email is still the most appropriate, but ensure the tone remains professional and not overly persistent.

Content: restate your interest in the position, inquire about the timeline for a decision, and offer to provide additional information if needed.

Example: "I wanted to follow up regarding the [Job Title] position. I'm still very interested and was wondering if there's a timeline for the next steps. Please let me know if you need any further information from me."

Further follow-up: demonstrate value (if another week passes with no response)

Method: continue using email to maintain consistency.

Content: share a recent development or achievement that further strengthens your candidacy or demonstrates your fit for the role. It could be a recent accomplishment, a relevant article, or a new certification.

Example: "I hope this email finds you well. I recently completed a [specific project/certification] that aligns well with our discussion on [specific topic]. I believe this further emphasizes my fit for the [Job Title] role. I'm looking forward to any updates you might have."

Final follow-up: seek closure (after another week or a given timeline has passed)

Method: email remains the standard, but ensure your tone is understanding and respectful.

Content: express understanding that priorities can shift but indicate that you're seeking closure for your own career planning.

Example: "I understand that priorities can change, and timelines can be extended. If the recruitment process for the [Job Title] role has shifted or if the role has been filled, I'd appreciate a brief update for my own planning. Thank you for considering my application."

Move on

If there's still no response after your final follow-up, consider focusing your efforts on other opportunities. It's essential to balance persistence with respecting the company's decision-making process.
