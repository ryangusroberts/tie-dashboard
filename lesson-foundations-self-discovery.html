The intention of self-discovery

This is the part where we slow down to speed up. There are two things you need to do in this section before we move forward in your executive career journey:

  • Understand who you are now.
  • Determine who you need to become to take the next step in your career and your life.

For your path forward to appear, you must get clear on where you are going. Otherwise, you are drifting along the current of life.

"If you don't know where you're going, any road will take you there." — Lewis Carroll

Take your time going through this section. Find a quiet place and give yourself some quality undisturbed time to do this part. The more introspective and centered you are when you do this, the more you will get out of it. One of the worst outcomes of this program would be to get a promotion into a role you hate. So let's do the work now to understand where you really want to go in your career.

Understanding who you are now

We have to be honest with ourselves about who we are today. None of us are likely the highest, best, most self-actualized versions of ourselves. We can all stand to improve significantly. And that's okay. It's normal to have a version of ourselves that we aspire to become. This exercise is not about judging our current self or our environment. It's to define our starting point at the beginning of a significant transformation.

How well do you know your self?

It's helpful to have a benchmark for where you are beginning. Give yourself a few moments to take this questionnaire. This will assess your current level of self-awareness and can be revisited later to get a sense of how far you've come in your self-discovery journey.

Self-reflection

After going through this questionnaire, reflect on your thoughts about each of these questions. Which ones made you feel uncomfortable? Which ones did you honestly answer "I have no idea," and how can you begin to understand that aspect of your self? Knowing what you don't know about yourself is the most important part of this process. Use your Executive Journal to record your thoughts. You might want to revisit this journal entry again after we go through the Executive Skills section.

Envisioning your executive self

Next, we want to spend some time really trying to visualize our fully, self-actualized, EXECUTIVE version of ourselves. This is the version of yourself that you catch glimpses of. This is the version of yourself you know is in there, and why you are in this program right now.

When envisioning your executive self

Take a moment to envision the future you currently want for yourself, in as much detail as you can. Imagine what it would be like to live this future life. Imagine what you're doing, where you are, how you feel, who you're with. Use prompts to help bring the details of this vision forward. Focus your attention on how your body feels as you try to embody this future life.

Becoming an executive gives you agency to lead in your own way. You may be drawn to leadership because you want to escape the pain of the work you do now once and for all. And that's okay. But it's important to understand whether you are drawn to leadership, or pushed away from a field you no longer love. In both cases, moving to the executive level will help, but be sure to go there with the right intentions in your heart, or you will be just as miserable.

Take some time to journal on this last question in your Executive Journal. And record any other thoughts that come up for you throughout this exercise. Especially the uncomfortable and inconvenient ones. Those are your true, actualized self finally getting a chance to speak up.
