Deploying Your Executive Brand

Put your brand to work, online and in real life, without tripping the alarms at your current job.

What you'll learn: becoming who you will be; testing and deploying your brand online and in real life (IRL); and how to get started with written content on LinkedIn.

Becoming who you will be

Now that you've defined your Executive Brand, it's time to put it out there for the world to see. Your brand is based on your key accomplishments, strengths and skills, and where you want to drive your career. Here we're going to start 'wearing' that executive brand as much as possible, as if it were a part we are playing in a movie.

Every time you go into the office, a trade show or industry event, and go online, you're going to wear this persona. This is a character you are going to portray. You don't have to be 100% confident that this persona is actually YOU just yet; we just need to make sure it's a version of yourself that you CAN and WANT to grow into.

To become the executive version of yourself, you have to act like an executive first. Action comes before belief. In other words, play the part to feel like you own the part.

Testing & deploying your brand

There are two ways we will deploy your executive brand: In Real Life (IRL) and Online.

In Real Life (IRL)

To deploy your executive brand in person, you're going to need to practice acting the way you will act when you take a new executive role. You probably did something like this without realizing it when you went from high school to college (or military, work, etc.), and when you went from individual contributor to manager. You're going to go through that kind of transformation again. It will feel uncomfortable. That's normal.

Your goal is to get as comfortable being the executive version of yourself as you can, as quickly as you can, so I suggest trying on your executive persona in "throwaway situations." Throwaway situations are like when you're at the restaurant, the grocery store, checking in/out of a hotel, etc. Places where you interact with people in a one-time, transactional way.

For example, go to a different coffee shop than you normally go into. Before you walk in, take a deep breath, and embody all of the mindsets, beliefs, and confidence you believe an executive version of yourself should and will have 10 years from now. Carry yourself the way you would imagine the CEO of a Fortune 100 company would carry themselves.

I highly recommend Toastmasters as a place where you can test this persona out as well. The whole Toastmasters community is there to literally practice public speaking, so take them up on the opportunity to do so. Remember that this practice is important so that you don't feel goofy when you go into your first executive interview. Be willing to be bad at something so you can get good at it.

Online

Speaking in your executive brand online is pretty straightforward. The content you write, share, and react to will speak volumes of who you are. It's a little easier to change how you show up online than it is in real life, so here you just slowly feather in content and commentary that comes from your new executive presence.

For example, if you already post a lot of content, simply add content related to your new persona over the next 3-4 weeks. If you don't post a lot of content (or none at all), you'll want to post something maybe once a week for two weeks, then twice a week for a few weeks, then more, and so on.

You can throw a yellow (or even red) flag for your current employer if you hop on LinkedIn, dust everything off, and start actively writing and updating your profile after years of inactivity. So it's best to slowly ease in to your content over the course of a month or two if you've been dead silent on LinkedIn for years.

How to get started with written content on LinkedIn

Start with comments

Your first content on LinkedIn should be comments on other people's posts. You can thoughtfully engage on posts about topics that are of interest to you. It doesn't have to be someone you know, you can search LinkedIn for articles about interesting topics. Commenting is lightweight, low-risk content that generally isn't seen by many and lets you ease into content gently, slowly. I recommend commenting on posts at least twice a week, and at least 2-3 posts each time.

Post about your current role

You can also repost content about your current company. This is by far the safest way to go from zero to hero engagement on the platform, Marketing will love you and HR won't worry about you. It also demonstrates what you do, your area of focus, etc.

How to not freak out your HR or manager

The most compelling reason I can think of to have an active brand on LinkedIn is because so many people only work on their LinkedIn when they're ready to quit. How many times have you seen a co-worker tidying up their LinkedIn, getting a bunch of recommendations, updating their photo, and then within 3 months they are gone? Turns out going from zero engagement to maximum engagement freaks out employers.

If you are currently employed and have hardly ever posted content on LinkedIn, you will want to take your time easing into content creation. You can start by commenting on other people's content, and slowly add in reposting some things from your own business or about your industry. After a few weeks of this you can add some short posts and thoughts, and then after a while you can include longer-form posts.

Slowly graduate to posts of your thoughts

As you build confidence in the comments section and begin to occasionally show up on your HR's radar, you can slowly weave in posts about your thoughts on things. As an introvert, you might want to start with simple and not very polarizing points of view. You can be as bland or bold as you feel comfortable being. Again, your content should be about the work you love, your experience and skills (without divulging company secrets), and trends you see in the industry. We'll talk more later about how to build a content calendar, but as you go through your day, start thinking about your thoughts in terms of "would this be a good piece of content?"

Content cadence

In the beginning, take things slow. If you are going from zero activity, post comments for 2-3 weeks, followed by some reposts or shout-outs about your current employer. After about a month you should be able to begin posting your own thoughts without tripping any alarms.

Actions to take: pick your executive persona and try it on in a throwaway situation this week. Start commenting on LinkedIn (2-3 posts, twice a week). Add a repost about your current role or industry. After a few weeks, weave in your own short posts, then longer-form thoughts.
