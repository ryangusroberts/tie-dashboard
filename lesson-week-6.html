LinkedIn as your primary acquisition system

This training provided by Ryan Roberts

Purpose: to create a simple, scalable, and repeatable marketing funnel where your clients are.

Your SSI score: the baseline

LinkedIn measures how effectively you use the platform through the Social Selling Index (SSI), rated 0 to 100 across four pillars:

PillarWhat LinkedIn measures
Professional BrandProfile completeness, content published, follower growth
Finding the Right PeopleSearches, profile views, use of filters
EngagementComments, shares, message response rates
RelationshipsConnections added, acceptance rate, network depth

The benchmark is 70+. Sales professionals at SSI 70+ generate 45% more opportunities and are 50% more likely to hit quota. For consultants, a 70+ score signals active thought leadership. Right now, open LinkedIn, go to linkedin.com/sales/ssi, and drop your score in the chat.

Your SSI score tells you exactly where your leverage is. Don't try to improve everything at once. Find your weakest pillar and attack it first.

Your profile is a landing page, not a resume

Your LinkedIn profile has one job: answer, in under 10 seconds, "Can this person solve my specific problem?" If it reads like a resume, the answer is unclear. If it reads like a landing page, the answer is yes.

Element 1, headline. The most heavily weighted element in the algorithm and the first thing your ICP reads. Weak: "Senior Director | 15 Years Experience | B2B Expert." Strong formula: [Who You Serve] + [Transformation You Deliver] + [Proof/Credibility], for example "Turning corporate expertise into $20K/month consulting income | Fractional Strategy Executive."

Element 2, about section. Opening hook (a question or bold statement that makes your ICP feel seen), who you serve and what you do in one sentence, proof points with numbers, how you work in plain language, and one clear call to action.

Element 3, featured section. The most underused real estate on LinkedIn. Use it for case studies with specific results, client testimonials (video is best), published frameworks, and your highest-performing posts.

Element 4, banner image. Your billboard. Include a direct message to your ICP with a clear next step, not a logo or a stock photo.

Element 5, custom URL and contact link. Set a custom URL and add your calendar link in Contact Info so prospects can book without asking.

Document drop
  • LinkedIn Authority Audit, score yourself honestly on all five elements. Where you're a 1 or 2 out of 5 is your homework target.

The volume connection strategy

For B2B consultants and fractional executives, the goal is to build a large, targeted network fast. Your profile does the qualifying, your content does the nurturing, and your outreach starts the conversation. The system: target 200 connection requests per week (LinkedIn's limit), 40 per day, 5 days per week, with no note.

ApproachWeekly requestsAcceptanceNew connections
Volume, no message20072%~108
Personalized messages5054%~36

Your profile does the screening. When someone receives your request they either accept or visit your profile, and both outcomes are a win. Build your target list by filtering on job title (your ICP), company size, industry, and "posted on LinkedIn in the last 30 days." Send 40 requests (under 10 minutes a day), then let your content work.

What not to do: don't pitch the day they accept, don't send a "thanks for connecting" with a calendar link, and don't use the connection request itself to sell. The 10% exception: personalize only for warm connections who engaged with your content, referred prospects, or 2nd-degree connections with a strong mutual contact, keeping this under 10% of weekly activity.

Content that attracts your ICP

Content is your prospecting engine running 24/7. Every post should either educate your ICP or position you as the obvious solution to their problem.

TypeExample hook
Pattern Recognition"Analyzed 40 consulting engagements. Here's what the ones that failed had in common."
Contrarian Take"Most fractional executives are solving the wrong problem."
Case Study"Client came to me at $0 consulting revenue. 90 days later: $18K/month."
Framework / How-To"The 3-question framework I use to price every engagement."
Behind-the-Scenes"I lost a $40K deal last month. Here's what I learned."

Hook formula, the first line is everything. Bold statement, pattern, question, or curiosity gap. Never start with "I'm excited to share..." or "I've been thinking about...", these kill reach in the first second.

Frequency: 2 to 5 posts per week. Two exceptional posts beat seven mediocre ones every time.

Algorithm mechanics (2026): engagement in the first 60 to 90 minutes is the primary signal, so post Tuesday to Thursday, 8 to 10 AM in your ICP's timezone. Reply to every comment within the first hour (+35% reach), put external links in the first comment (not the post body), and comment on your own post at 8 and 24 hours to reactivate distribution. Carousels and in-feed text outperform external link posts. Check your SSI every Monday and attack your weakest pillar.

Content systems and social media strategy

This training provided by Tom Cox

One ICP, one Offer, one Channel, until one million dollars.

Platform selection: where does your ICP actually live?

All platforms work. Choose based on your familiarity with the platform and the style of content you want to create. You do not need to be everywhere. Dominate one platform, then add a second only when the first is running systematically. For B2B consultants and fractional executives, LinkedIn is non-negotiable as the primary platform. Pick LinkedIn first, then one secondary platform that matches how you naturally communicate (writing, speaking, or video), and build the second channel only after LinkedIn is on autopilot.

PlatformBest forContent type
LinkedInB2B buyers, executives, decision-makersThought leadership, frameworks, case studies
YouTubeLong-form authority, evergreen searchTutorials, deep-dives, client stories
PodcastHigh-trust, long-form nurtureConversations, frameworks, interviews
Newsletter & EmailOwned audience, highest conversionWeekly value, curated insights

Content pillar framework

Content pillars solve the blank page problem. They tell you exactly what to post and give your audience a reason to keep following you. Know your pillars, and rotate ideas through them.

Pillar 1, Expertise: what you know. Frameworks, insights, and patterns from your work, where you educate your ICP. Pillar 2, Proof: what you've done. Client results, case studies, transformations, where you build credibility. Pillar 3, Perspective: what you think. Contrarian takes, observations, predictions, where you build a point of view. Pillar 4, Story (optional but high-engagement): why you do it. Your background, failures, and path, where you build trust and connection.

The test: each pillar should generate at least 5 post ideas immediately. If it doesn't, it's too broad.

Document drop
  • Content Pillar Map, name your 4 pillars and write 5 post ideas under each. That's 20 posts, a month of content.

The repurposing system: create once, use many ways

Efficient operators don't create more, they repurpose what they already made. One idea becomes five pieces: the core insight (raw material), a LinkedIn long-form post (primary distribution), a carousel version (higher share rate), a short-form version (for your secondary platform), and a newsletter inclusion (your owned audience). That's how you maintain 2 to 5 posts per week without spending 20 hours creating content.

Build the repurposing habit from week 1. Don't just create content. Create assets.

Funnels and your marketing stack

There are two types of content: organic and paid. You only need one right now. Organic content is content you create with no paid reach; if it's good, the platform amplifies it. Lay down a base of content around your pillars so people who find you can absorb more and adopt your worldview. Tom's definition of good: content you would watch yourself if you were your ICP-1. Paid traffic is when you pay to put content in someone else's feed, and it should have a very clear call to action. You do not need ads until you have absolutely exhausted your organic outreach.

The funnel has three phases. Top of funnel: people become aware of you (cold leads) through your organic content. Middle of funnel: people evaluate your offering (warm leads seeing you as a thought leader); nurture with an email list and low-ticket items. Bottom of funnel: people convert from lead to prospect and will need demos, testimonials, and collateral to move forward.

Hard-learned rules: people float between funnel stages, sometimes for years; every platform changes everything about itself every 2 to 3 years; you only need one funnel; the middle of funnel is the hardest to get right; and stuffing the top doesn't necessarily mean more reach the bottom.

Key marketing questions answered

Website vs. just a LinkedIn profile? When people want to see you're a business and not just an individual. For fractionals and consultants this could be well into the future; for coaches, sooner.

Minimum viable funnel for $20K/month? Content on LinkedIn, a lead magnet, a Calendly link, and a Stripe link. You need more when you want to do $300K/month.

When should I run ads? When you have more capacity for sales calls than prospects, but ONLY after you've built a solid organic content machine, delivered a solid offer multiple times, received several great testimonials, and can allocate 10% of revenue to ads.
