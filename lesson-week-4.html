Identity shift and imposter syndrome

This training provided by Ryan Roberts

Purpose: address the psychological shift from "exploring" to "operating," and provide tools for managing imposter syndrome.

The identity shift when someone pays you

Before payment: "I'm exploring this idea. I'm testing. I'm curious." After payment: "I own a business. Someone trusted me with their money. Now I have to deliver."

This shift feels sudden, but it's REAL. You've gone from aspiration to operation.

The 3 phases of founder identity

PhaseMindsetRisk
Exploring"I'm trying things. No commitment yet."Staying here forever. Never launching.
Operating"I run a business. I have clients. I deliver."Imposter syndrome. Overdelivering. Burnout.
Leading"I lead a business. I build systems. I delegate."Control issues. Identity tied to doing, not leading.

Where are you now? Most of you are still exploring, some have just entered operating. That's exactly where you should be.

Why imposter syndrome spikes after the first sale

If you can't recall summiting Mt. Stupid, you might be standing on top of it.

Imposter syndrome intensifies AFTER the first sale because now the stakes feel a lot more real.

The paradox: competent people feel like imposters, incompetent people don't. Your imposter syndrome is actually evidence of your self-awareness. The internal dialogue: "What if I can't deliver? What if they find out I'm not that good? What if they ask for a refund?" The reframe: your client didn't buy your confidence. They bought your expertise. They're not paying for you to feel certain, they're paying for their outcome.

The 5 imposter syndrome techniques

  1. Document your wins. Keep a running list. Reference it when doubt creeps in.
  2. Competence inventory. List 10 things you know how to do that your client doesn't.
  3. Reality check questions. "What's the evidence that I CAN'T deliver? What's the evidence that I CAN?"
  4. External perspective. "If my best friend had my credentials, would I think they were a fraud?"
  5. Action over analysis. Imposter syndrome lives in the head. Action kills it.

Ryan: "I built a defense company while sailing the Caribbean. Every time we landed a new government contract, I'd think: who am I to do this? Then I'd remember, they chose us because we deliver. Your client chose you. Trust their judgment."

Delivery MVP and onboarding

This training provided by Tom Cox

Simple scales, fancy fails. A tactical framework for delivering excellent results without overcomplicating things.

The MVP delivery principle

MVP means minimum viable product. What's the LEAST you can deliver to get your client the promised outcome?

First-time founders tend to OVERBUILD their delivery system.

The danger of overbuilding: you don't know what your client actually needs yet, you'll burn out trying to deliver 20 modules when they need 4, and complexity creates confusion, not transformation. The right approach: build the simplest possible path to the outcome, 4 to 6 steps max for your first client, and let the client's real needs shape what you add later.

Think of your first client as a CO-CREATOR, not just a customer.

The Build 6-step delivery framework

This is a notional approach to an 8-week coaching or consulting engagement. Every offer will be different, but 8 weeks is usually enough time to do great discovery and transformation work, while being short enough to make everyone feel okay about entering into the engagement.

StepWhat happensDuration
1. Intake & BaselineUnderstand where they are, set expectationsWeek 1
2. Core Framework DeliveryTeach your main methodologyWeeks 2-4
3. Implementation SupportHelp them apply itWeeks 4-6
4. Milestone CheckReview progress, adjust approachWeek 6
5. Advanced ApplicationDeepen the work based on their progressWeeks 7-8
6. Completion & Next StepsCapture testimonial, discuss continuationWeek 8+

Intake & baseline (Week 1). Get absolute clarity about where the client currently is. This is why Foundations is primarily about self-discovery. Both you and they know where they are starting.

Core framework delivery (Weeks 2-4). Define the transformation and the transformation mechanism, for example the Whole Executive Framework or the Revenue First Framework.

Implementation support (Weeks 4-6). The core of the training part of the transformation. Whether coaching or consulting, this is where you do the most work shifting mindsets and making sure your customer adopts the new way of doing things.

Milestone check (Week 6). The point where customers start to wonder if they have transformed. Show them how far they've come, remind them of the journey behind them, and recalibrate expectations going forward.

Advanced application (Weeks 7-8). Lean in toward the end of the engagement. Help them see what's now possible with the framework and implementation you've provided.

Completion & next steps (Week 8+). Begin negotiating a follow-on deal, typically an ascension offer to solve new problems created by solving the first one.

Your framework will evolve. Start simple, and let the first client's experience shape version 2.0.

Client onboarding essentials

The first 48 hours matter. How you onboard sets the tone for the entire engagement. The more dialed in the first 48 hours are, the more confidence your client will have in your ability to deliver.

ElementWhat to include
Welcome messageCongratulate them. Confirm next steps. Set expectations.
Intake formGather baseline info: goals, obstacles, timeline, communication preferences.
Communication rhythmHow often will you meet? How do they reach you between calls?
First winWhat's the FIRST small win you can help them achieve in Week 1?
Expectations documentWhat you will do. What they must do. Timeline. Boundaries.
The first win in Week 1 creates belief. Belief creates momentum. Momentum creates results. Results create testimonials. Testimonials create more clients.

Showing up as authority

Authentic confidence is not fake confidence. You don't need to know everything, you need to know more than them about this specific problem. Authority equals competence plus empathy, so demonstrate both. If you don't know something, say so and find the answer. That's how you build trust.

Clients would rather trust what you say than believe you know everything, only to find out you don't know what you're talking about.

Working session: build your delivery

Part I, delivery framework build (20 min). Open the Delivery MVP Template. 10 minutes of silent work drafting your 6-step delivery framework, then 10 minutes of hot seats sharing and receiving feedback. Hot seat questions: can your client actually achieve the outcome in this structure? Is there anything you can cut, and what's the minimum viable solution? What's Step 1, and how do they know they're making progress? Common mistakes to watch for: too many steps (more than 6 is too complex), vague milestones ("improve mindset" vs. "complete leadership assessment"), and no clear first win in Week 1.

Part II, onboarding script roleplay (10 min). Pair up. One person runs through their onboarding (welcome, expectations, first meeting agenda). Feedback: was it clear? Did you know what to do next? Did you feel taken care of? Questions for the "client": do you know exactly what happens next? Do you feel confident this will work? What questions do you still have?

Part III, first client coaching (5 min). For those who have closed: when does your client start, what's your onboarding plan, and what's the first win you'll help them achieve? For those who haven't: what's blocking the close, when's your next conversation, and what do you need from us before that call?
